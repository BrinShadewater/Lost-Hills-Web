Re: Depth sounder giving errors near the monitoring array

The monitoring array does put out a low-frequency acoustic signal as part of its survey operation. This is a known thing and it can interfere with some consumer depth finders. Nothing to worry about. The buoy placement will be adjusted after the survey wraps up and everything should go back to normal.

Permits are still valid. Fishing from the south pier is fine. Just give the buoys some room.

Re: What exactly is the “Continuity Grid”?

I've lived here twelve years and I can tell you that nobody really gets a straight answer on the Continuity Grid. The council voted to approve it back in ’91, I think. The vote was unanimous, which is unusual because Councilman Redd never agrees with Councilwoman Park on anything.

What I've been told informally: it connects the city's power substations, the hospital's systems, the dispatch network, and the CityNet server infrastructure into a shared failover ring. If any one node goes down, the others maintain continuity. Think of it like a circuit that stays closed no matter which part you cut.

What I've never gotten a straight answer on: what the third relay in the ring is. Everyone says "and the Shadewater campus" but the campus is outside city limits and shouldn't be part of the civic grid.

Re: What exactly is the “Continuity Grid”?

Hi folks — I work in Public Works. Happy to answer what I can.

The Continuity Grid is real and it works. It keeps civic services running through power events, infrastructure failures, and (in its full configuration) certain communication disruptions. It's been tested in partial configuration several times since ’91. No full activation has been necessary.

The third relay is the Shadewater campus — it has a large independent power generation and storage capacity and is a logical choice for a failover node. The specific details of the relay architecture are under a standard civic infrastructure nondisclosure.

Full activation is scheduled to coincide with the SASC closing session on May 21. Mayor Cordell will say a few words. It should be routine.
